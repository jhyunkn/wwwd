A 2,300-year-old solar observatory, rebuilt at model scale to test how a landscape teaches a body to read time.

Context

Chankillo, on the Peruvian coast, is among the oldest known solar observatories — thirteen towers along a ridge that calendar the year by where the sun rises and sets between them. The investigation treats the site less as a ruin than as an instrument: a piece of built landscape that organizes perception, threshold, and territorial observation.

Method

Physical model-making across topography, paired with redrawn observatory plans and sections, and time studies of light and occupation in the common space between the towers.

Artifact

A model installation — lookout and tower models framing territorial observation, built at a scale that lets a viewer move around the site and read it in sequence.
