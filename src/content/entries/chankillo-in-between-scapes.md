Chankillo is not useful to WWWD as a beautiful ancient reference alone. Its pressure is larger: a landscape where architecture, astronomy, memory, and territorial order once operated as one system.

This investigation asks what architecture can do when the site is already a machine for reading time. The proposal works through thresholds and lookout conditions rather than a single iconic object. Walls, observatories, dwellings, common spaces, and sectional cuts become a way to test how people move from terrain into evidence, from observation into orientation, and from ruin into renewed public meaning.

The point is not to restore certainty. It is to make relationships visible enough to act on. Chankillo becomes a first WWWD Investigation because it carries the full chain: a claim, a method of inquiry, spatial artifacts, and an external consequence for how the site can be interpreted.
