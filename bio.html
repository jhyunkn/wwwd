Jun is a New York–based architect, educator, and researcher whose work examines architecture as a system of relationships between material, construction, behavior, culture, and place.

Educated at the City College of New York and the Yale School of Architecture, Jun has developed a practice grounded equally in design inquiry and the realities of making. His professional work engages architecture across scales of representation, coordination, material resolution, and construction, while his teaching at the City College of New York explores the relationship between design, building technology, and research.

Rather than treating architecture primarily as the production of objects, Jun is interested in the conditions that produce them: how materials acquire cultural meaning, how construction decisions shape perception and behavior, how buildings participate in larger social and economic systems, and how architecture can reveal qualities of a place that might otherwise remain unseen.

This inquiry is developing through WWWD (What Would We Do?), an independent research and spatial practice conceived as a platform for architecture, investigation, and cultural production. Its work begins not with a predetermined formal language, but with questions—seeking forms of architecture that emerge from careful observation, material intelligence, and the particular conditions of each situation.

Across practice, teaching, and research, Jun’s work is ultimately concerned with architecture as an act of interpretation: understanding what already exists, identifying the forces operating beneath the visible, and determining what should be made visible through design.
