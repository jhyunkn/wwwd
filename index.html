    In Between Scapes

    An observatory at Chankillo, Peru, investigating interstitiality — how can space commit to a singularity without excluding one or the other?

    Aerial site plan of the Chankillo observatory project, Peru

    In-Side In

    A renovation bridging Portsmouth Square and the Hilton Hotel in San Francisco’s Chinatown — expressing the multiplicity of Asian American identity, toward an architecture of hybridity.

    Atlas of event and space collage, Portsmouth Square San Francisco

    Glass Pavilion

    An investigation on material utility over performance — glass at Seagram Plaza, CNC-milled to express the tectonic values of the material.

    CNC-etched glass study for the Glass Pavilion

    Jook Bu-In

    Deconstructing Korea’s bamboo wife — re-orienting an indigenous weaving technique toward a tactile communication that celebrates intimacy in the public realm.

    The bamboo wife, Jook Bu-In, in use

    Kitchen within Boundaries

    Cooking as a medium of design research — after the pandemic’s abandonment of the kitchen: can the disposition of value exist prior to architecture?

    Collective cooking session, Kitchen within Boundaries research

    East River SDC

    A social disciplinary center in the East River — overhauling the punitive system into a rehabilitative one, challenging the prison typology as a whole.

    Aerial plan of the East River Social Disciplinary Center archipelago

    Aaron Davis Facade

    A facade as stage for Aaron Davis Hall — the curtain’s symbolic and functional tactility, a skin that bisects interior and exterior.

    Model of the Aaron Davis Hall facade proposal, CCNY

    Brownsville Dwelling

    A micro-collective dwelling for Brownsville, Brooklyn — home, work, and communal gathering toward self-sufficiency: not exclusive inclusion, but inclusionary exclusion.

    Brownsville neighborhood strategy map, Brooklyn

    Pyung Sang

    A series of benches in varied sizes, promoting activities beyond seating — Korea’s traditional social bench, reimagined. With Dash Marshall.

    Pyung Sang flat bench, bent plywood seating

    Royal Crescent

    A precedent study of John Wood the Younger’s 1774 crescent at Bath — thirty units behind a unified facade, an early rus in urbe, still relevant for housing in the city.

    Model of the Royal Crescent, Bath
